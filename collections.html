The Collections

Seven collections

We organise what we import the way an Italian kitchen is organised — by what a thing is for. Each collection is a shortlist of houses whose work we can stand behind, with the region and the denomination named, because those are the only facts that matter once the crate is open.

Olive Oils and Vinegars

Oli e Aceti

Oil is the first thing we were exacting about and the thing we argue about most. We buy single-estate extra virgin from groves we have walked, pressed within hours of picking and bottled in dark glass with the harvest on the label, because an oil without a date is an oil with something to hide.

Alongside it: balsamic aged in a battery of woods rather than hurried along with caramel, and wine vinegars sharp enough to be worth the name.

Regions
Puglia · Liguria · Toscana · Umbria · Sicilia
Denominations
Terra di Bari DOP · Riviera Ligure DOP · Toscano IGP · Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOP
How we handle it
Harvest-dated, dark glass or tin, stored away from light and heat

Cheeses

Formaggi

Cow, sheep, goat and buffalo — from mozzarella still warm in its whey to a thirty-six month Parmigiano that crunches. We taste by lot rather than by label, because two wheels stamped the same month out of the same dairy are not the same cheese.

Wheels and forms arrive whole and are cut to order. Nothing is portioned in advance for the sake of shelf life.

Regions
Emilia-Romagna · Campania · Sardegna · Piemonte · Lombardia
Denominations
Parmigiano Reggiano DOP · Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP · Pecorino Sardo DOP · Gorgonzola DOP · Taleggio DOP
How we handle it
Temperature-controlled from dairy to warehouse; cut to order, never pre-portioned

Salumi

Salumi e Insaccati

Whole-muscle and ground, cured on mountain air, salt and patience. The geography is the recipe here: the same leg hung in Parma and in Friuli becomes two different hams, and the difference is the wind.

We work with cellars that still age to time rather than to a schedule, and we buy legs on the bone wherever the customer can use them that way.

Regions
Emilia-Romagna · Friuli-Venezia Giulia · Toscana · Umbria · Calabria
Denominations
Prosciutto di Parma DOP · Culatello di Zibello DOP · Prosciutto di San Daniele DOP · Finocchiona IGP · Soppressata di Calabria DOP
How we handle it
Cellar-aged 14 to 36 months; whole, boned or sliced to order

Pasta and Grains

Pasta e Cereali

Bronze dies and slow drying, from durum grown on the Tavoliere. The rough surface that comes off a bronze die is not a flourish — it is the reason the sauce stays on the pasta instead of pooling in the bowl.

With it: rice for risotto that holds its centre, stone-ground polenta, and flours milled for a purpose rather than for everything.

Regions
Puglia · Campania · Piemonte · Veneto
Denominations
Pasta di Gragnano IGP · Riso di Baraggia Biellese e Vercellese DOP
How we handle it
Bronze-drawn, dried 24 to 60 hours at low temperature

Truffles and Funghi

Tartufi e Funghi

The white truffle of Alba in autumn and the black of Norcia in winter, bought through the men who dig them and flown while they still smell of the ground they came out of. Truffle is the least forgiving thing we handle: a day is the difference between remarkable and merely expensive.

Out of season, porcini dried in the Apennines, and preserves built on real truffle rather than synthetic aroma.

Regions
Piemonte (Alba) · Umbria (Norcia) · Marche · Abruzzo
Species
Tuber magnatum pico · Tuber melanosporum · Tuber aestivum
How we handle it
Air-freighted in season, weighed on arrival, allocated by the day

Wines and Aperitivi

Vini e Aperitivi

Classical houses and small growers side by side: Nebbiolo from Barolo, Sangiovese from Montalcino, Corvina from the Valpolicella, and the volcanic reds coming off Etna that have rearranged how people think about Sicilian wine.

Alongside the bottles, the vermouth, amari and bitters that open and close an Italian meal. Wine and spirits are supplied through licensed import and distribution partners in each state.

Regions
Piemonte · Toscana · Veneto · Sicilia · Campania
Denominations
Barolo DOCG · Brunello di Montalcino DOCG · Amarone della Valpolicella DOCG · Etna Rosso DOC
How we handle it
Reefer containers at cellar temperature; licensed channels only

Conserves and Sweets

Conserve e Dolci

The jarred half of an Italian pantry, which is the half most often cheapened: San Marzano tomatoes grown on the Agro Sarnese-Nocerino and peeled by hand, anchovies under salt, artichokes and peppers put up in oil worth eating on its own.

Then the sweet end — Bronte pistachio, Piedmont hazelnut, honey, and the festival breads in their seasons.

Regions
Campania · Sicilia · Piemonte · Calabria
Denominations
Pomodoro San Marzano dell'Agro Sarnese-Nocerino DOP · Pistacchio Verde di Bronte DOP · Nocciola Piemonte IGP
How we handle it
Glass where glass is right; nothing with added colour or aroma
