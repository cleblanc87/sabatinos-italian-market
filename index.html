Our Undertaking

Three things we do not compromise

I

Bought at origin

We buy from the families who make what we carry. No broker stands between their press and our manifest, which is why we can name the mill, the grove and the harvest behind everything we bring in.

II

Protected by name

Where a denomination exists, we honour it. DOP and IGP are not decoration but a contract about geography, method and time — the difference between Parmigiano Reggiano and something merely grated.

III

Handled without a gap

Cheese and cured meat begin to change the moment they leave the cellar. Our consignments travel temperature-controlled from producer to port to warehouse, by the shortest route we can arrange.

The Collections

What we bring across

Seven categories, each assembled the same way: a shortlist of houses whose work we can stand behind, and nothing on the list to fill a gap.

Olive Oils and Vinegars

Oli e Aceti

Single-estate extra virgin pressed within hours of picking, and vinegars from Modena aged in wood rather than hurried with colour.

Cheeses

Formaggi

Cow, sheep, goat and buffalo, from young and milky to crystalline and thirty months deep. Cut to order, never pre-portioned for shelf life.

Salumi

Salumi e Insaccati

Whole-muscle and ground, cured on mountain air and patience — prosciutto, culatello, finocchiona, capocollo, guanciale, lardo.

Pasta and Grains

Pasta e Cereali

Bronze-drawn and slowly dried, from durum grown on the Tavoliere. Rice for risotto, polenta, and flours milled for their purpose.

Truffles and Funghi

Tartufi e Funghi

Alba white and Norcia black in their seasons, alongside porcini dried in the Apennines and preserves made without perfume.

Wines and Aperitivi

Vini e Aperitivi

Small growers and classical houses, plus the vermouth, amari and bitters that begin and end an Italian meal.

Sourcing

Twenty regions, each with its own argument

Italy does not cook as one country, and it does not sell as one either. An oil from Puglia and an oil from Liguria are not two grades of the same thing; they are two different ideas about what oil is for.

So we buy regionally. We spend our time where a thing is actually made — in the caseificio at the morning milking, at the mill during the raccolta, in the cellar where the hams hang — and we come home with a shortlist rather than a catalogue. When a producer has a poor year, we would rather carry nothing from them than carry something they are not proud of.
